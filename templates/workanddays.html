History

Features

  • First work written for a personal occasion: advice to his brother Perses;
  • Themes: labour and justice;
  • Two sections: moral and agriculture advice:
    • In first part, Hesiod denounces "gift-devouring kings," praises retributive Justice, and explains why the world is in such a sorry state (through the myth of the Ages of Man and the myth of Pandora);
    • In the second part, he speaks of his land, offers advice while also describing the sweltering heat of summer and the biting cold of winter;
    • Linked by the central idea that people must work honestly, uphold justice, and remain faithful to traditional moral norms of good neighborliness.

Gods

Human behavior is under the constant surveillance of Zeus, who appears in the poem as the guardian of justice and the judge of those who violate it.

Style

  • Shaped by oral performance;
  • Repetition;
  • Fixed formulas (lines with identical endings);
  • Frame structure;
  • Simple syntax;
  • Almost no vivid images;
  • Hexameter: the epic verse (traditionally intended to celebrate heroes and battles—becomes) is used for entirely new content that is ideologically opposed to that of the Homeric epics.
