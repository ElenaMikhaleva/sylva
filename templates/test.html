Sociolinguistics — variation

Sept 3 · Linguistics

Labov's core finding: linguistic variation isn't random noise, it's socially structured. The same speaker shifts forms depending on formality, audience, and self-monitoring.

Definition

Covert prestige — the positive value a non-standard form holds within a group precisely because it signals in-group identity, even though it carries no status outside that group.

Key terms

  • Sociolect — variation tied to a social group rather than a region.
  • Prestige form — the variant associated with higher social status.
  • Covert prestige — a non-standard form valued within a group precisely because it isn't standard.
Key info

Labov's department store study (1966) is the go-to citation for this — it's what most of the seminar reading builds on.

Question to revisit

How much of "code-switching" is conscious versus automatic? Look at Bell's audience design model next.

"The vernacular is the most systematic form of speech."
— Labov, quote directly later

Standard deviation

Statistics

How spread out a set of values is from the mean — the square root of the variance, which keeps the units the same as the original data.

Formula
σ = √( Σ(xᵢ − μ)² / N )
Population version — divide by N−1 instead of N for a sample.
Compare to variance — this is just its square root, in the original units.
