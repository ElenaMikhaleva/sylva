Cenozoic

History · Paleontology

The Cenozoic Era is the third of the three major eras of the Phanerozoic Eon. Lasting nearly 66 million years from 66 to today, it represents The Age of Mammals. Below are Periods of Cenozoic.

Pleistocene

2,580 – 11,7 Kya
  • The cooling trend that started Mid-Eocene caused Ice ages. There were at least four separate glaciation periods;
  • Desiccation in Africa, the Sahara, Namib, and Kalahari deserts appeared;
  • 100,000 years ago ended one of the worst droughts in Africa, which led to the expansion of primitive humans;
  • Animals evovled (mammoths, giant ground sloths, dire wolves, sabre-toothed cats, Homo sapiens);
  • At the end of Pleistocene, a major extinction wiped out much of the world's megafauna, including some of the hominid species (Neanderthals). All the continents were affected, but Africa to a lesser extent. It still retains many large animals (hippos).

Holocene

11.7 Kya – today
  • All of human history.

Miocene

23 – 5.3 Mya
  • The Tethys Sea closed with the creation of the Arabian Peninsula, leaving only remnants as the Black, Red, Mediterranean and Caspian Seas. This increased aridity;
  • Grasses spread further, dominating a large portion of the world, at the expense of forests.95% of modern seed plants families were present by the end of the Miocene;
  • Kelp forests evolved, encouraging the evolution of new species (sea otters);
  • Apes evolved into 30 species.

Pliocene

5.3 – 2.6 Mya
  • Climate: savannas were continuing to spread across the world, Indian monsoons, deserts in central Asia, the beginnings of the Sahara desert. The world map has not changed much since;
  • Australopithecus evolved in Africa, beginning the human branch;
  • The Isthmus of Panama formed, and animals migrated between North and South America during the Great American interchange;

Paleocene

66 – 56 Mya
  • The continents began to take their modern shape;
  • Climate: warming trend, with jungles reaching the poles;
  • After The Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event large herbivores became extinct, which permitted the spread of dense but usually species-poor forests;
  • The oceans were dominated by sharks as the large reptiles that had once predominated were extinct. Archaic mammals filled the world (creodonts).

Eocene

56 – 34 Mya
  • Climate: warming trend, with jungles reaching the poles;
  • In the Mid-Eocene, the Antarctic Circumpolar Current between Australia and Antarctica formed. It caused a global cooling effect;
  • Early primates, whales and horses evolved. At the top of the food chains were huge birds (Paracrax);
  • Mammals grow to mammoth proportions (whales, fully aquatic by that time);
  • Ended with Eocene–Oligocene extinction event.

Oligocene

34 – 23 Mya
  • Expansion of grasslands which had led to many new species to evolve (the first elephants, cats, dogs);
  • Mammals continued to grow larger.
