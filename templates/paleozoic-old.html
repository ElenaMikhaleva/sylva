Paleozoic

History · Paleontology

From Greek palaiós (ancient) + zōḗ (life).

The Paleozoic Era is the first of the three major eras of the Phanerozoic Eon. Lasting nearly 290 million years from 538 to 251 Mya, it represents the dramatic transition from soft-bodied marine organisms to complex, fully terrestrial ecosystems dominated by plants, giant arthropods, and early vertebrates. Cambrian

Кембрий
538 – 485 Mya

From Latin Cambria (Wales), where rocks of this age were first studied.

  • Tectonics: supercontinent Pannotia broke up; major landmasses later recombined into Gondwana;
  • Flora: primitive non-vascular land plants started appearing on land;
  • Fauna: Cambrian Explosion — a dramatic expansion and diversification of animal life in the oceans. Armored arthropods (trilobites, radiodontids) and early shelled cephalopods (orthocones) dominated the fauna;
  • Fauna: mollusks and other invertebrates began evolving protective shells and exoskeletons;
  • Species: the first vertebrates appear.

Ordovician

Ордовик
485 – 444 Mya

From Latin Ordovices (Celtic tribe), because rock strata were first studied extensively in Wales.

  • Flora: algae dominated oceans, primitive plants continue spreading on land;
  • Fauna: rapid evolution of primitive nautiloids, jawless fish, corals, and bryozoans.
  • Fauna: primitive arthropods made temporary trips out of the water into coastal areas and tidal pools;
  • Climate: a rapid ice age dropped temperatures worldwide.
  • Glaciers locked up water, draining shallow inland seas where most life lived. Climate shifts brought low-oxygen, toxic bottom waters onto continental shelves. It caused The Late Ordovician mass extinction (LOME) wiped out nearly 85% of marine species (most trilobites, many bryozoans).
LOME, 445 Mya

Silurian

Силур
444 – 419 Mya

From Silures (Celtic tribe), because rock strata were first studied extensively in Wales.

  • Fauna: jawed fish and freshwater species appeared;
  • Land: fully terrestrial life established, including early fungi, arachnids, myriapods (centipedes/millipedes) , and hexapods;
  • Land: plants(ferns and club moss ancestors) evolved vascular systems to transport water and nutrients upward.
jawed fish, 439 Mya
land animals, 435 Mya
vascular plants, 425 Mya

Devonian

Девон
419 – 359 Mya

From Devon, England, where rocks from this period were first studied.

  • Age of Fish. Oceans and reefs flourished with diverse fish, early sharks, armored placoderms, sea scorpions, and cephalopods;
  • Land: woody ferns and the earliest seed plants evolved during this period; shrub-like forests grew by the Middle Devonian;
  • Tetrapod Evolution: fish developed sturdy, fleshy fins that evolved into four limbs. Appeared first land vertebrates;
  • Near the end of the Devonian, 70% of all species became extinct in a sequence of mass extinction events, collectively known as the Late Devonian extinction.
tetrapods, 390 Mya
Late Devonian Extinction, 372 Mya

Carboniferous

Карбон
359 – 299 Mya

From Latin carbō (coal) + ferō (carry) due to the vast coal beds formed.

  • Dense swamp forests covered land, that created coal beds; plants developed deep root systems, expanding inland;
  • Land plant expansion caused high oxygen levels;
  • High oxygen permitted giant insects (like Meganeura) and arachnids;
  • Early reptiles (amniotes) evolved hard-shelled eggs, allowing full independence from water.

Permian Period

Пермь
299 – 251 Mya

From Perm, where rocks from this period were found.

  • Gondwana, Euramerica and Siberia merged into Pangaea;
  • Extreme conditions such as polar ice caps and inland deserts limited the extent of plant life, favoring drought-resistant gymnosperms (conifers)
  • Amphibious tetrapods and synapsids (mammal-like reptiles like Dimetrodon) diversified where plants grew;
  • Mass extinction (Great Dying), caused by Siberean Traps. This extinction was the largest in Earth's history and led to the loss of 95% of all species of life.
