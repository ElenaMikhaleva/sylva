Mesozoic

History · Paleontology

The Mesozoic Era is the second of the three major eras of the Phanerozoic Eon. Lasting nearly 186 million years from 252 to 66 Mya, it represents The Age of Reptiles. Below are Periods of Mesozoic.

Early Cretaceous

145 – 100 Mya
  • Dinosaurs continued to be abundant.

Late Cretaceous

100 – 66 Mya
  • Climate became cooler;
  • The first flowering plants evolved;
  • Dinosaurs thrived, new species (Tyrannosaurus, Ankylosaurus, Triceratops) appeared;
  • Mammals diversified, marsupials and placental mammals appear;
  • Mosasaurs filled the role of now-extinct ichthyosaurs;
  • At the end of Cretaceous, volcanic eruptions were poisoning the atmosphere. As this was continued a large meteor smashed into Earth, creating the Chicxulub Crater and causing The Cretaceous–Paleogene extinction event (K–Pg), the fifth and most recent mass extinction event. Every living thing with a body mass over 10 kilograms became extinct (all non-avian dinosaurs).

Jurassic

Юра
201 – 145 Mya
Jurassic, illustration
  • Humid climate made the world was warm and partially tropical. Conifer savannahs made up a large portion of the world's forests;
  • Plesiosaurs, ichthyosaurs and ammonites dominated the seas;
  • Dinosaurs, pterosaurs and other reptiles dominated the land;
  • True mammals were present, but remained small.

Early Triassic

251 – 247 Mya
  • a hot and arid period in the aftermath of the Permian Extinction;
  • Tetrapod reptilians evolved into large aquatic predators (Ichthyosaurs);
  • On land, the first true archosaurs appeared.

Middle Triassic

247 – 237 Mya
  • Pangaea started to break up;
  • Phytoplankton, coral, crustaceans and other marine invertebrates recovered from the Permian extinction;
  • On land, reptiles continued to diversify, conifer forests flourished, the first flies appeared.

Late Triassic

237 – 201 Mya
  • Climate: warm and arid with strong monsoons. A 2 million years-long wet season which transformed the arid continental interior into lush forests;
  • The first true dinosaurs (Eoraptor) and pterosaurs appeared;
  • Many large reptilian competitors to the dinosaurs were wiped out by the Triassic–Jurassic mass extinction (TJME).
