Paleozoic

History · Paleontology

From Greek palaiós (ancient) + zōḗ (life).

The Paleozoic Era is the first of the three major eras of the Phanerozoic Eon. Lasting nearly 290 million years from 538 to 251 Mya, it represents the dramatic transition from soft-bodied marine organisms to complex, fully terrestrial ecosystems dominated by plants, giant arthropods, and early vertebrates. Permian

Пермь
299 – 251 Mya

From Perm, where rocks from this period were found.

Permian Period, illustration
  • Gondwana, Euramerica and Siberia merged into Pangaea;
  • Extreme conditions such as polar ice caps and inland deserts;
  • Amphibious tetrapods and synapsids (Dimetrodon) diversified where plants grew;
  • Mass extinction (Great Dying), caused by Siberian Traps. This extinction was the largest in Earth's history and led to the loss of 95% of all species of life.
Pangea, 299 Mya
Great Dying, 252 Mya

Carboniferous

Карбон
359 – 299 Mya

From Latin carbō (coal) + ferō (carry) due to the vast coal beds formed.

  • Dense swamp forests covered land, that created coal beds; plants developed deep root systems, expanding inland;
  • Land plant expansion caused high oxygen levels;
  • High oxygen permitted giant insects (like Meganeura) and arachnids;
  • Early reptiles evolved hard-shelled eggs, allowing full independence from water.

Devonian

Девон
419 – 359 Mya

From Devon, England, where rocks from this period were first studied.

  • Age of Fish. Oceans and reefs flourished with diverse fish, early sharks (Cladoselache), placoderms, sea scorpions, and cephalopods;
  • Tetrapod evolution: fish developed sturdy, fleshy fins that evolved into four limbs;
  • Land: woody ferns and the earliest seed plants evolved; shrub-like forests grew by the Middle Devonian;
  • Near the end of the Devonian, 70% of all species became extinct in a sequence of mass extinction events, collectively known as the Late Devonian extinction.
Late Devonian Extinction, 372 Mya

Silurian

Силур
444 – 419 Mya

From Silures (Celtic tribe), because rock strata were first studied extensively in Wales.

Silurian Period, illustration
  • Fauna: jawed fish (Placoderm) and freshwater species appeared;
  • Land: fully terrestrial life established, including arachnids, myriapods, and hexapods;
  • Land: plants evolved vascular systems to transport water and nutrients upward (Cooksonia).

jawed fish, 439 Mya
land animals, 435 Mya
vascular plants, 425 Mya

Ordovician

Ордовик
485 – 444 Mya

From Latin Ordovices (Celtic tribe), because rock strata were first studied extensively in Wales.

Ordovician Period, illustration
  • Flora: algae dominated oceans, primitive plants, fungi continue spreading on land;
  • Fauna: rapid evolution of primitive nautiloids;
  • Fauna: primitive arthropods made temporary trips out of the water into coastal areas and tidal pools;
  • Climate: a rapid ice age dropped temperatures worldwide;
  • Glaciers locked up water, draining shallow inland seas where most life lived. Climate shifts brought low-oxygen, toxic bottom waters onto continental shelves. It caused The Late Ordovician mass extinction (LOME) wiped out nearly 85% of marine species.
sea scorpions, 480 Mya
LOME, 445 Mya

Cambrian

Кембрий
538 – 485 Mya

From Latin Cambria (Wales), where rocks of this age were first studied.

Cambrian Period, illustration
  • Tectonics: supercontinent Pannotia broke up; major landmasses later recombined into Gondwana;
  • Flora: primitive non-vascular land plants started appearing;
  • Fauna: Cambrian Explosion — a dramatic expansion and diversification of animal life in the oceans. Armored arthropods (trilobites, radiodontids) and early shelled cephalopods dominated the fauna;
  • Fauna: mollusks began evolving protective shells and exoskeletons;
  • Species: the first vertebrates appear.
