Paleozoic

History · Paleontology

From Greek palaiós (ancient) + zōḗ (life).

The Paleozoic Era is the first of the three major eras of the Phanerozoic Eon. Lasting nearly 290 million years from 538 to 251 Mya, it represents the dramatic transition from soft-bodied marine organisms to complex, fully terrestrial ecosystems dominated by plants, giant arthropods, and early vertebrates. Cambrian

Кембрий
538 – 485 Mya

From Latin Cambria (Wales), where rocks of this age were first studied.

Cambrian Period, illustration
  • Tectonics: supercontinent Pannotia broke up; major landmasses later recombined into Gondwana;
  • Flora: primitive non-vascular land plants started appearing;
  • Fauna: Cambrian Explosion — a dramatic expansion and diversification of animal life in the oceans. Armored arthropods (trilobites, radiodontids) and early shelled cephalopods dominated the fauna;
  • Fauna: mollusks began evolving protective shells and exoskeletons;
  • Species: the first vertebrates appear.

Ordovician

Ордовик
485 – 444 Mya

From Latin Ordovices (Celtic tribe), because rock strata were first studied extensively in Wales.

Ordovician Period, illustration
  • Flora: algae dominated oceans, primitive plants continue spreading on land;
  • Fauna: rapid evolution of primitive nautiloids, jawless fish, and corals;
  • Fauna: primitive arthropods made temporary trips out of the water into coastal areas and tidal pools;
  • Climate: a rapid ice age dropped temperatures worldwide.
  • Glaciers locked up water, draining shallow inland seas where most life lived. Climate shifts brought low-oxygen, toxic bottom waters onto continental shelves. It caused The Late Ordovician mass extinction (LOME) wiped out nearly 85% of marine species.
LOME, 445 Mya

Silurian

Силур
444 – 419 Mya

From Silures (Celtic tribe), because rock strata were first studied extensively in Wales.

Silurian Period, illustration
  • Fauna: jawed fish and freshwater species appeared;
  • Land: fully terrestrial life established, including early fungi, arachnids, myriapods (centipedes/millipedes) , and hexapods;
  • Land: plants (ferns and club moss ancestors) evolved vascular systems to transport water and nutrients upward.
